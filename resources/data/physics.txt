**Questions on Motion and Measurement**

Physics is the science of how matter and energy behave, from the smallest particles to the largest structures we can observe.

Experimentation settles the question. When two explanations disagree, a good measurement shows which one fits, and a new explanation has to account for every result that came before it.

A surprising claim calls for strong proof, and a single measurement is rarely enough. Other people need to be able to repeat the result, with their own equipment.

A ball thrown straight up slows down, stops for an instant and falls back, because gravity acts on it the whole time.

A heavy truck and a small car rolling at the same speed do not stop in the same distance on the same brakes, as the truck carries more energy.

**Questions on Units and Errors**

Scientific units use powers of ten, so that moving between meters and kilometers takes no more than moving a decimal point.

Precision tells how close repeated measurements are to each other, and accuracy tells how close they are to the true value. A careful experiment needs both.

An answer in physics has a size and a unit, so the first check is whether the unit makes sense. A speed given in kilograms shows a mistake somewhere.

A rough estimate before a calculation is worth the minute it takes. If the result is a thousand times larger, something has gone wrong, and the science is in finding out where.

Rounding too early adds errors that grow at each step, so a value is carried with extra digits and rounded once, at the end.
